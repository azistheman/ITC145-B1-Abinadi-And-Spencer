The History of TVs: Costs of TVs

By Spencer & Abinadi

Televisions started out as heavy, expensive CRT boxes with knobs and large speakers. Early sets cost hundreds of dollars then — a huge sum in today’s terms. Small consumer comforts like early remotes (for example the "Lazy Bones") added convenience but were themselves costly when introduced.

Color television and portable sets were major innovations that initially carried high price tags. Companies invested heavily in research; the first color sets and portable models sold for hundreds to thousands of dollars. Over time prices fell, making these features mainstream.

Through the 1990s and 2000s TVs evolved from bulky CRTs to flat panels. Plasma and early flat-screen models were breakthrough technologies but extremely expensive at first. The LED era and improvements in manufacturing dramatically lowered prices while increasing sizes and picture quality.

Smart TVs and internet-connected "content" sets arrived in the late 2000s, bringing streaming to the living room. High-end breakthroughs — large 4K debut models and experimental technologies like 3D — were priced for early adopters and usually dropped quickly or faded if consumers didn’t embrace them.
